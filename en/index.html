How we grade

Each layer is graded on its own; there’s no overall score. Only checks marked “Official docs” or “Common practice” count; hypotheses and notes don’t. Blocking training crawlers costs nothing: that’s your call.

  • Can they get in: S, every AI search crawler got in; A, all get in but some hit a challenge or a thinner page, or the live test didn’t finish and only robots.txt was checked; B, some AI search crawlers are blocked; C, all are blocked, a silent block was found, or the homepage is noindex. A robots.txt that returns 5xx or can’t be reached counts as “disallow everything” under RFC 9309.
  • Can they read it: S, nothing to improve; A, one item; B, two or three, or a non-critical problem; C, indexing or snippets are blocked, the text needs JavaScript, or four or more items need work.
  • Can they cite it: five signals (structured data, organization, site name, share card, semantic tags). S all five, A four, B two or three, C one or none; a JSON-LD parse error, or FAQ markup whose questions aren’t on the page, caps it at B.

How to fix

Amazon CloudFront is answering these requests, most likely through AWS WAF. The Bot Control rule CategoryAI blocks AI crawlers even when they are verified. Override it to Count, then re-block only the training crawlers with your own rule.

How we test, and what it can’t tell you

We request your homepage from a data-center IP as a normal browser, with a Googlebot user agent, as an unknown bot, and with each AI crawler’s user agent. We don’t come from the crawlers’ own networks, so a site that verifies crawler IP addresses may treat the real crawler differently. The control groups separate a rule aimed at AI crawlers (high confidence) from a general bot rule (medium) or an IP-verification rule (low). To be certain, check your CDN or WAF logs for requests from the crawler’s published IP ranges. Methodology

FAQ

Questions

Isn’t robots.txt enough?

robots.txt is only a request. Your CDN, firewall or a security plugin decides what actually gets through, and it can reject a crawler that robots.txt welcomes. That is the silent block we test for.

Will allowing AI search let my content be used for training?

No. OpenAI and Anthropic use separate crawlers for search and training, and Apple and Google give training its own robots.txt name (Applebot-Extended, Google-Extended). Perplexity says PerplexityBot isn’t used to train models. So you can allow OAI-SearchBot and block GPTBot, for example. Our guides show how on each platform.

How accurate is a test from a data center?

Real crawlers come from their own IP ranges, which we can’t use. So every test runs with control groups (a normal browser, Googlebot and an unknown bot) and each result carries a confidence level.
